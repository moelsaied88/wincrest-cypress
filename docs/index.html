A neighborhood built for small explorers

Lakewood Forest is 2,617 homes under a canopy of oaks in northwest Harris County. This is a guide to what surrounds it.

Inside Lakewood Forest

The neighborhood itself

Lakewood Forest is 2,617 homes on large lots under mature oaks, with landscaped medians, streets quiet enough to learn to ride a bike on, and one of the lowest HOA fees in northwest Houston. Everything below sits within about two miles of the front door.

7 min · 2.2 mi

Lakewood Residents' Club

A 50-meter temperature-controlled pool with a diving board, a separate wading pool for the little ones, and lifeguards on duty whenever it is open. There is a playground with a climbing structure, sand volleyball, four lighted tennis courts and two pickleball courts, and a summer swim team that most neighborhood kids seem to end up on. It sits right beside Faulkey Gully, so the trail is a few steps from the gate.

Worth knowing up front: this is a separate members-only club, not a Lakewood Forest HOA amenity. Living in the neighborhood does not grant access — households join and pay dues, currently $592 a year or $50 a month.

Membership required — $592/year per household · Courts open year-round; swim team runs in summer

Parks, playgrounds and the library — close enough for after school, and close enough to leave early when someone melts down.

9 min · 3.4 mi

Matzke Park

The Be An Angel playground here is barrier-free, so ramps and ground-level activities let children of different abilities play together, and side rails run the length of every bridge and platform — which matters a great deal when a three-year-old is running. There is a rope spider-web climber they make straight for, a rock wall paired with a slide, swings and a sway bench, all over soft wood-fiber surfacing. The wider 19-acre park adds a butterfly and sensory garden, two picnic pavilions, restrooms and a paved trail.

Free · Open daily, 7am–10pm

Water, and where to find it

Houston summers make this the section that matters. These are all public — no resident key fob required.

25 min · 13.8 mi

Zube Park Splash Pad

The splash pad sits on the Becker Road side of Zube Park and runs May through September, free and unstaffed, with a playground right beside it for when someone needs a dry break. The rest of the 225-acre Precinct 4 park gives you nearly three miles of paved trail, picnic areas, fishing and a disc golf course. The part your kid will actually talk about afterward is the Houston Area Live Steamers' miniature railroad, whose working steam and diesel model trains run on scheduled public days.

Free · Splash pad open May 1 – Sept 30

Somewhere to go when outside is not an option

Indoor play for the afternoons when the heat index wins.

7 min · 2.7 mi

Fun N Play Indoor Playground

The centerpiece is a three-story slide, backed up by a side-by-side triple slide and an ocean-themed ball pit with a banana canoe to flop into. Crawlers and brand-new walkers get their own padded Kiddie Corner, so a three-year-old and a baby sibling can both be occupied at the same time. Open play needs no reservation between 10 and 5, two adults come in free with each paid child, and everyone — grown-ups included — has to wear socks.

$11.99/child weekdays, $12.99 weekends · Year-round · Ages infant–8

32 min · 17.8 mi

John Paul Landing Park

This is the closest thing to a proper day in nature within half an hour of the house, and it rarely feels crowded. Three separate playground clusters, a paved trail loop a five-year-old can manage on a balance bike, and a lake big enough that small children ask whether it is the sea. The Environmental Education Center a few minutes up the road is open Tuesday to Saturday and runs free family programming worth building a morning around.

Free
